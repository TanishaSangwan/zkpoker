## Findings

[P0] **1. `bet()`/`settle_table()` never checked `table_settled`, permanently locking any bet placed after settlement**

`Class: STATE-GUARD-MISSING` · `cairo/src/lib.cairo` (`bet()`, `settle_table()`) · Confidence: 92 · Severity: Critical

**Description**
`table_settled` was written once, permanently, at the end of `settle_table`, and correctly checked in `reclaim_stalled_bet` — but neither `bet()` nor `settle_table()` itself ever read it. Two consequences: (1) `bet()` still pulled real funds into `table_pot`/`seat_contributed` for an already-settled table, and since `reclaim_stalled_bet` is permanently blocked once `table_settled` is true, that late bet became recoverable only if the same dealer voluntarily settled again — the exact abandoned-dealer scenario `reclaim_stalled_bet` was built to solve, resurrected with no timeout fallback. (2) `settle_table` had no guard against running a second time for the same table, so a dealer (who may also hold a seat) could redirect a freshly-landed pot to winners/notes of their own choosing after the table's real first settlement. Independently confirmed by two of the four partition agents (Math/Economics at conf 92, Storage/Trust at conf 80 — kept the higher).

**Status: FIXED.** Both `bet()` and `settle_table()` now assert `!table_settled` at entry, reverting with `ALREADY_SETTLED`.

**Required Tests**
- Settle a table, then call `bet()` again on it — assert it reverts with `ALREADY_SETTLED` (previously succeeded and inflated `table_pot`).
- Settle a table once, then call `settle_table` again — assert it reverts with `ALREADY_SETTLED` (previously succeeded as a no-op on a zeroed pot, but would have silently redistributed if new bets had landed).

[P0] **2. `settle_table` excluded from the shared `reentrancy_lock`, letting a dealer-controlled token reenter it mid-`bet()`/`reclaim_stalled_bet()`/`privacy_invoke()`**

`Class: CROSS_FUNCTION_REENTRANCY` · `cairo/src/lib.cairo` (`settle_table()`) · Confidence: 80 · Severity: Critical

**Description**
`bet()`, `reclaim_stalled_bet()`, and `privacy_invoke()` all take `reentrancy_lock` around their external ERC20 calls, but `settle_table()` — which mutates the same `table_pot`/`pending_payout`/`table_settled` state — never checked that lock. `table_token` has no allowlist, so a caller who both creates a table (its `table_dealer`) and supplies a hook-capable token as that table's currency controls a callback point inside `transfer_from`. When a seat calls `bet()`, the malicious token's `transfer_from` could reenter `settle_table()` before returning; `get_caller_address()` in that reentrant call is the token contract itself, arranged by the attacker to equal `table_dealer`, so the dealer check passed even without the lock. The reentrant settle would finalize the pot as it stood *before* the in-flight bet's contribution landed, permanently blocking `reclaim_stalled_bet` for every seat at that table via `table_settled` — while the just-added bet was never included in any payout.

**Status: FIXED.** `settle_table` now asserts `!reentrancy_lock` at entry (reverting with `REENTRANCY`). It doesn't need to also *hold* the lock across its own execution, since it makes no external calls itself — nothing can reenter *during* `settle_table`, the risk was always `settle_table` reentering *into*.

**Required Tests**
- A mock ERC20 whose `transfer_from` reenters `settle_table` mid-`bet()` call — assert the reentrant call reverts with `REENTRANCY` (and, pre-fix, demonstrate it currently succeeds with `table_settled == true` while the in-flight bet's contribution is unaccounted for).
